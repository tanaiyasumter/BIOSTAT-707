Overview

This analysis/report builds predictors from the first 48 hours of Intensive care unit (ICU) data in the PhysioNet Challenge 2012 set-a (4000 stays). Our outcome is in-hospital death.It compares three models against two clinical benchmarks, Simplified Acute Physiology Score I (SAPS-I) and (Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) using the same 5-fold cross-validation (CV).

2.1 The modeling table

The modeling table is built from Checkpoint 1 wide table(count, first, last, minimum, maximum and mean of each variable over the 48 hours) and has one row per stay. It is joined to In-hospital_death from Outcomes-a.txt by RecordID. Checkpoint 1 wide table used raw values, so here it is rebuilt from the cleaned long table. Every model gives one prediction per stay, so it can be compared directly with SAPS-I and SOFA. The code checks that no Record ID is duplicated.

Of the 4000 stays, 554 (13.9%) end in hospital death.

The cleaning decisions here are the same from Checkpoint 1: -1 is now missing, pH outside 6.5 to 8.0 and temperature outside 25 to 43 degrees became missing, heart-rate and blood-pressure values of 0 were left unchanged (zeros seen rarely). 1,894 stays had no height and 16 of the 2,106 recorded heights were impossible (outside 100 to 230 cm) so Height was dropped and we kept Weight (missing in 326 stays).

SAPS-I and SOFA are used only as benchmarks so where -1 was coded was set to missing. Three stays have no time series but remain with the 0 counts and missing summaries.

2.2 Predictors

After dummy-coding ICU type, and adding four missingness flags, the model sees 36 columns. Only data from hours 0–48 are used, Length_of_stay and Survival are not predictors because they are only known after the outcome (leakage). SAPS-I, SOFA and RecordID are excluded as predictors as well. ICUType and Gender are categorical.

17 of the 30+ time series variables were left out: ALP, ALT, AST, DiasABP, FiO2, HCT, K, Mg, Na, NIDiasABP, NIMAP, NISysABP, PaCO2, PaO2, SaO2, SysABP and Urine. The predictors chosen, cover the main organ systems that drive ICU mortality using the same kinds of measurements that SAPS-I and SOFA are built from. They were not left out because of missingness (most were measured for over 65% of stays). They were left out to keep the model small and interpretable. For most variables I used the worst value in the 48 hours because it would help signal to sicker patients. Variables were measured for under 30% of stays (5–28%) (troponin I, troponin T, cholesterol and respiratory rate) enter only as counts, along with lactate and mechanical ventilation. These counts are included because a test or treatment is ordered when a clinician is worried, so whether it was done carries information. These 17 variables weren’t left out due to missingness because most were measured for over 65% of stays. They were dropped to keep the model small and interpretable. Height was dropped for being about 47% missing.

2.3 Cross-validation design

5-fold cross-validation was used on set-a with a seed set to 707 and folds stratified by death (111, 111, 111, 111 and 110 deaths per held-out fold). The same folds were used for every model to ensure that performance comparisons are not confounded by different train/test splits. The folds aren’t clustered and each stay is treated (independently) as one row. All preprocessing steps (imputation, scaling, and dummy‑coding) were refit within each training fold. Hyperparameters were tuned using nested cross‑validation (inside each outer training portion, choosing the configuration with the lowest log loss before evaluating on the outer held‑out fold). The Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUROC) is reported for each curve with its mean and standard deviation.

2.4 Model 1: regularized logistic regression

The Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) penalty was used to obtain a sparse, interpretable model with one coefficient per predictor. The penalty parameter was tuned over 20 values from 0.0001 to 0.1 and the selected values (0.0009–0.0026) are similar across folds. Mean AUROC was 0.832 (SD 0.009). 25–34 of the 36 predictors were retained per fold, with this, the model is close to standard logistic regression. Twenty predictors (age, GCS_last, lactate, BUN etc.) appeared in all five folds.

Per-fold AUROCs for the LASSO are shown in the comparison table in Section 2.7. The mean was 0.832 (SD 0.009).

rec <- recipe(death ~ ., data = d_a) |>
  update_role(row_id, new_role = "id") |>                    # keep the ID, never use it
  step_indicate_na(MAP_min, pH_min, Bilirubin_max, Albumin_min) |>  # "was it measured" flags
  step_impute_median(all_numeric_predictors()) |>            # medians from the training fold
  step_impute_mode(all_nominal_predictors()) |>
  step_dummy(all_nominal_predictors()) |>                    # sex and ICU type to 0/1 columns
  step_zv(all_predictors()) |>                               # drop constant columns
  step_normalize(all_numeric_predictors())                   # same scale for the penalty

dim(bake(prep(rec), new_data = NULL))
spec <- logistic_reg(penalty = tune(), mixture = 1) |> set_engine("glmnet")

grid <- tibble(penalty = 10^seq(-4, -1, length.out = 20))

lasso_res <- lapply(1:5, function(k) {

    auc = roc_auc(pred, death, .pred_1)$.estimate,

2.5 Model 2: GAM

A Logistic Generalized Additive Model (GAM) was fit with smooth terms (penalized splines, smoothness chosen by REML) on 10 continuous predictors and ICUType. The predictors are: Age, Weight, GCS_last, HR_max, Temp_min, BUN_max, WBC_max, Platelets_min, HCO3_min and Glucose_max.

Why were these predictors chosen: These 10 continuous predictors were chosen based on clinical grounds, covering age, nutrition, neurological status, heart, temperature, kidney, white cells, platelets, bicarbonate and glucose, and most of them were kept in all five LASSO folds.

Missing values are filled with the training-fold median, taken from the training fold only.

Per-fold AUROCs for the GAM are shown in the comparison table in Section 2.7. The mean was 0.826 (SD 0.013).

gam_vars <- c("Age", "Weight", "GCS_last", "HR_max", "Temp_min",
              "BUN_max", "WBC_max", "Platelets_min", "HCO3_min", "Glucose_max")

gam_formula <- as.formula(paste("death01 ~ ICUType +",
                                paste0("s(", gam_vars, ")", collapse = " + ")))


gam_res <- lapply(1:5, function(k) {
  tr <- as.data.table(analysis(folds$splits[[k]]))
  te <- as.data.table(assessment(folds$splits[[k]]))
  tr[, death01 := as.integer(death == "1")]
  te[, death01 := as.integer(death == "1")]
  for (v in gam_vars) {                       # medians from the training fold only
    med <- median(tr[[v]], na.rm = TRUE)
    set(tr, which(is.na(tr[[v]])), v, med)
    set(te, which(is.na(te[[v]])), v, med)
  }
  fit <- mgcv::gam(gam_formula, family = binomial, data = tr, method = "REML")
  p <- as.numeric(predict(fit, newdata = te, type = "response"))
  list(oof = data.table(row_id = te$row_id, fold = k, model = "gam",
                        pred = p, death = te$death01),
       auc = as.numeric(pROC::auc(te$death01, p, levels = c(0, 1), direction = "<")))
})

or_curve <- function(var, ref) {
  x <- seq(quantile(gam_all[[var]], 0.01), quantile(gam_all[[var]], 0.99), length.out = 100)
  g <- as.data.frame(gam_all)[rep(1, length(x) + 1), ]
  g[[var]] <- c(x, ref)
  Xp  <- predict(gam_fit, newdata = g, type = "lpmatrix")
  Xd  <- sweep(Xp[-nrow(Xp), , drop = FALSE], 2, Xp[nrow(Xp), ])
  est <- as.vector(Xd %*% coef(gam_fit))
  se  <- sqrt(rowSums((Xd %*% vcov(gam_fit)) * Xd))
  data.frame(x = x, or = exp(est), lo = exp(est - 1.96 * se), hi = exp(est + 1.96 * se))
}

plot_or <- function(var, ref) {
  ggplot(or_curve(var, ref), aes(x, or)) +
    geom_ribbon(aes(ymin = lo, ymax = hi), alpha = 0.2) +
    geom_line() +
    geom_hline(yintercept = 1, linetype = "dashed") +
    scale_y_log10() +
    labs(x = var, y = "Odds ratio of death", subtitle = paste("Reference:", var, "=", ref))
}

2.6 Model 3: tree ensemble

Per-fold AUROCs for the random forest are shown in the comparison table in Section 2.7. The mean was 0.835 (SD 0.012)

Here a random forest (ranger, 500 trees) was used. Missing values were imputed with the training-fold median, plus the same four missingness flags. The tuning grid was mtry (predictors tried per split) in {3, 6, 10} by min_n (minimum stays per leaf) in {10, 30, 60}, which is 9 combinations. They were chosen by log loss with the same inner 5-fold CV inside each outer training set.

rec_rf <- recipe(death ~ ., data = d_a) |>
  update_role(row_id, new_role = "id") |>
  step_indicate_na(MAP_min, pH_min, Bilirubin_max, Albumin_min) |>
  step_impute_median(all_numeric_predictors()) |>
  step_impute_mode(all_nominal_predictors())

rf_spec <- rand_forest(mtry = tune(), min_n = tune(), trees = 500) |>

  set_mode("classification")
rf_wf   <- workflow() |> add_recipe(rec_rf) |> add_model(rf_spec)

This Table shows the means and standard deviations across all folds and that all three models outperform both benchmarks. Mean AUROC was 0.832 for the LASSO, 0.826 for the GAM and 0.835 for the random forest, compared with 0.650 for SAPS-I and 0.623 for SOFA. The three models differ by at most 0.009, within their fold-to-fold SD, so none is clearly better.

The best model beats SAPS-I and SOFA by about 0.18–0.21 AUROC, many times the fold-to-fold SD (about 0.01–0.03).

I choose LASSO as the final model because it is the simplest, linear, and has lowest SD(0.009). LASSO was refit on all of set-a and saved.

SAPS-I and SOFA are fixed scores, not fitted to this data, whereas the models were trained on it. Five folds also give only a rough estimate of the SD.
